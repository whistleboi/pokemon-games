Route 12

With the Poké Flute from Mr. Fuji in your Bag, head south out of Lavender Town. Route 12 is a long pier running south, a favourite spot for anglers. A sleeping Snorlax lies right across the way south, and nothing gets past it until it wakes up.

The TM by the fence and the Iron lie in plain sight. The other three items are hidden: walk over their spots to pick them up.

Wake the Snorlax

Walk up to the Snorlax. Without the Poké Flute it won't budge. With the Flute, it wakes up and battles you. You can catch it or knock it out: either way the road south to Route 13 is open from then on.

Snorlax. A wild battle, about five levels above the route's own Pokémon. A second Snorlax sleeps on Route 16.

Route 12 Gate

The guard on the ground floor tells you Lavender Town is to the north and the pier stretches a long way south. Climb to the second floor, where a little girl is enjoying the view. She gives you TM274 Return, a move that grows stronger the more your Pokémon likes you. The binoculars look out over the pier and up at the Pokémon Tower.

Fishing House

The Fishing Guru's younger brother lives in the house on the pier. He loves fishing as much as you do, so he hands you his best rod, the Super Rod. It works anywhere there's water, and the biggest Pokémon bite on it.

Route 13

Past the Snorlax, the path turns west through patches of tall grass. It's packed with trainers, so heal up before you set off. Ditto turns up in the grass here for the first time.

One sign tells you to look to the left of it, because something is hidden there. It's right: a PP Up.

Route 16

There's another way down to Fuchsia: west out of Celadon City and down Cycling Road. Route 16 is where it starts. A second Snorlax sleeps here, and it wakes the same way, with the Poké Flute. Beat or catch it to reach Cycling Road. You also need a Bicycle to ride on to Route 17.

A quiet house

Just west of Celadon City, behind a small tree, a girl has a hideaway house with her Fearow. Few people ever find it. She gives you HM Fly, which takes you in a flash to any town you've already visited. You need the Thunder Badge to use it outside battle.

Cycling Road Gate

The guard explains that Cycling Road is downhill all the way and only for Bicycles. If you don't have one, a cyclist points you to the Bike Shop in Cerulean City. Upstairs, a young couple are setting off on a bike ride. Oak's other aide is here too: catch forty kinds of Pokémon and she gives you an Amulet Coin. While it's on your team, battles pay out double.

Route 17 (Cycling Road)

Cycling Road is one long downhill slope along the sea. The slope keeps pulling you south, and riding back up takes twice as long. Bikers and Cue Balls wait along the way. A sign reminds you to press WAIT if you want to brake and stay in one place on the slope.

Cycling Road is full of things people dropped. Five items are hidden along it, including a Rare Candy and a Max Revive.

Fuchsia City

Fuchsia City sits on Kanto's south coast. It's home to Koga's Gym and to the Safari Zone, with a small zoo of Safari Pokémon out front: Lapras, Kangaskhan, Chansey, Voltorb and Slowpoke. Routes 15 and 18 lead in from the east and the west. Later, with Surf, you can head south down Route 19.

Koga's daughter is out training with Poison Pokémon too. A boy who loves Kangaskhan teaches one of your Pokémon Substitute, a move that makes a decoy of itself. Like every move teacher in this game, he can also sell you the move as a TM ours.

{{tables:FUCHSIA_CITY}}

The Strength HM and the Rare Candy both come from the Warden's House (see below).

Fuchsia Gym

Koga's Gym is full of invisible walls. Koga stands in plain sight, but you have to feel your way to him. The Gym Guide says Psychic moves work well against his Poison Pokémon.

Koga's Weezing is his strongest Pokémon. Beat him for the Soul Badge and TM175 Toxic. The Soul Badge lets you use Surf outside battle.

Warden's House

The Safari Zone's Warden lives here, surrounded by Pokémon photos and fossils. He has lost his Gold Teeth, so all he can do is mumble and point toward the Safari Zone. Find them in the Safari Zone's west area and bring them back. He's so grateful that he gives you HM Strength, which pushes big boulders out of your way. You need the Rainbow Badge to use it outside battle.

A Rare Candy is tucked behind the boulder in his room. A boy outside can't reach it either. Push the boulder with Strength to get it.

Fishing House

The Fishing Guru from Vermilion City has a younger brother here, who also loves fishing. He gives you the Good Rod, which pulls up bigger Pokémon than the Old Rod.

Other houses

In the house on the west side, an old man turns out to be Bill's grandfather. He says Bill liked collecting things even as a boy. The woman there mentions that the Warden's teeth are false. The Move Deleter lives in town too, though he thinks your team is fine as it is. In the Safari Zone Office, the workers say the Warden is running a campaign: whoever reaches the far corner of the Safari Zone wins a prize.

Safari Zone

The Safari Zone is a big wild park north of Fuchsia City, home to Pokémon you won't find anywhere else in Kanto. Chansey, Kangaskhan, Tauros, Scyther, Pinsir, Rhyhorn and Exeggcute all live here, and Dratini lives in the ponds. It's split into four areas: the Center Area by the entrance, Area 1 to the east, Area 2 to the north and Area 3 to the west. Crossing a gate line takes you into the next area, and each area has its own Pokémon.

The Safari Game

Step up to the desk in the Safari Zone's entrance building in Fuchsia. The attendant takes $500, and you get 30 Safari Balls and 600 steps. If you don't have enough money, you're turned back.

  • You can't battle wild Pokémon here. When one appears, you catch it with MATH: answer the question to throw a Safari Ball.
  • Safari Balls catch better than Poké Balls (×1.5), and they are only for this visit.
  • Every step uses one of your 600. When your steps run out ("Ding-dong! Time's up!") or your last Safari Ball is thrown, the game is over and you're walked back out to Fuchsia.
  • The $500 pays for one visit. Come back out and the next visit costs another $500.

Headbutt trees use Kanto's shared tree tables and need the Rocky Helmet. FireRed has no Headbutt trees. Picking up items costs steps like any other walking, so plan your route.

Rest houses

Each area has a rest house where you can catch your breath. A scientist is studying which Pokémon live in which area. A fan in the north rest house says Dratini live in the ponds, so bring a good rod. In the west rest house, people say something shiny is lying in the grass nearby, and that the Warden lost his teeth out west.

The Gold Teeth

The Warden's Gold Teeth are lying in the grass in Area 3 (West). Pick them up and take them back to his house in Fuchsia City.

The Secret House

Keep going to the far west corner of Area 3 to find the Secret House. You're the first to make it this far, so the man inside gives you the prize: HM Surf. With Koga's Soul Badge, a Pokémon that knows Surf carries you across the water.
