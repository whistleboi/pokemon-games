Introduction

Orre is a dry land of sand, rock and wind, far from the green regions. Hardly any wild Pokémon live here, so trainers battle in big stadiums called Colosseums. Lately something is wrong: some Pokémon have had their hearts shut tight, and they attack anyone, even people. These are Shadow Pokémon, and a secret group called Cipher is making them.

You used to ride with Team Snagem, a gang that steals other trainers' Pokémon. On the night your story begins, you turn on them: you run out of their desert hideout with their prize, the Snag Machine, on your arm. The boss, Gonzap, shouts after you, the hideout goes up in flames, and you speed off across the desert on your motorbike. By dawn you reach a lonely diner at the edge of the sand: the Outskirt Stand.

There are two ways to get here:

  • A new game. Pick Orre from the region list, choose your look and type your name. Orre has no rival to name. The opening plays: the hideout, the escape and the ride to the Outskirt Stand. Then two Pokémon hop down from your bike's sidecar: Espeon and Umbreon. They grew up together as Eevee and have never been apart. You pick which one leads the way, and the other bounds after it and joins your team too.
  • From another region. Once you become the Champion of the region you're in, Mom wants to spend your prize money on a new home and asks where to move. Pick Orre (she only offers regions you haven't played yet). You arrive at the Outskirt Stand and pick your lead partner the same way. Orre keeps its own party, PC and Bag, so Espeon and Umbreon are your whole team to start with.

Either way, your Journal (in the MENU) always shows your next main mission, and a gold marker on the map points the way.

One journey, two stories

Orre's story follows two games, one after the other: Pokémon Colosseum, then XD: Gale of Darkness, set five years later. Parts 1 to 4 of this guide are the Colosseum half: you stop Cipher's first plan, win four Gym badges, and take on Cipher's leader at Realgam Tower. Then the story jumps five years ahead, and Parts 5 to 8 follow XD with four more badges. After eight badges the Orre League waits at Realgam Tower.

The places of the first half stay open after the time skip, so a Shadow Pokémon you missed can still be snagged later.

Shadow Pokémon and snagging

Most trainers in Orre keep their Pokémon to themselves, but Cipher's people carry Shadow Pokémon, and Cipher hands them out to others too. With the Snag Machine you can throw a Poké Ball at another trainer's Shadow Pokémon in the middle of a battle: weaken it first, as you would a wild Pokémon, then throw. Throw a Ball at an ordinary trainer's Pokémon and the trainer just knocks it away.

At first you can't tell which Pokémon are Shadow Pokémon. That changes when you meet Rui in Phenac City: she can see the dark aura around them, and while she travels with you, it shows in battle.

A Shadow Pokémon you snag fights for you, but its heart is still shut. It knows a Shadow move, and its own moves stay hidden until its heart starts to open. Its Heart Gauge has five bars. Each battle it takes part in opens it a little, and so does walking with it in your party. Now and then in battle a Shadow Pokémon loses control (Hyper Mode) and ignores what you ask: the CALL button, in place of RUN, calms it down. Its heart can be purified at the Relic Stone in Agate Village (Part 3), and it becomes an ordinary Pokémon again.

Each place's tables list the Shadow Pokémon you can snag there, and the trainer who has each one. Missed one? Many places offer SNAG SHADOW POKÉMON: a Cipher trainer sends out the next one on the place's list you haven't snagged, so you get another chance. A Shadow Pokémon marked "in a story scene" is only its own trainer's, though: snag it in that trainer's battle, because the Cipher trainer never brings it. Once you've snagged one of a place's Shadow Pokémon, and none is left for SNAG SHADOW POKÉMON, the place offers SNAG AGAIN: a straggler brings back one you've already snagged there, as often as you like.

How this guide plays

This guide is written for two settings in Options › Gameplay: Battle style: Overworld and Level cap: Story. The cap is on from the start; the battle style you switch on yourself.

Double battles

Almost every battle in Orre is a double battle: two of your Pokémon against two of the other trainer's, side by side. That's why you start with two partners. Every battle still works the usual way: pick a move and answer its math question to land it.

Overworld battles

On walked maps like Pyrite Cave and Mt. Battle, trainers spot you and walk over. Their words come up in the map's text box, they send out their Pokémon in front of them, and you fight right there on the map. In the trainer tables, "on the map" marks these. The important story battles always open the battle screen: Gym Leaders, Cipher's Admins and their bosses, the Colosseum tournaments, and people who stop you for a battle in a scene. So do the battles in towns. Each battle box says which.

The story level cap

Your Pokémon can't level past the next Gym Leader's best Pokémon plus two. At the cap they stop gaining Exp., and Rare Candies don't work. Win a badge and the cap moves up. Each part starts with a box giving the cap for that stretch.

Levels never rise to meet you: trainers always keep their own levels, so the levels in this guide's battle boxes are the ones you'll face, however strong your team is.

{{captable}}

The cap only counts how many badges you hold, so it works the same whichever Leader you beat first. After you become Champion, the cap follows your own team.

Poké Marts and prizes

Orre's Poké Marts sell from the same counter as every other region's, and the shelf grows with the Orre badges you hold. TMs join it as you earn badges too. One thing is missing at first: Orre's Marts sell no Poké Balls until you've beaten one of Phenac's Mystery Troop guards (below). From then on, Poké Balls are on the shelf like everywhere else.

{{mart}}

Trainers on the walked maps don't pay in Poké Balls: beating one gives you a healing prize that grows with your badges.

Outskirt Stand

The Outskirt Stand is an old train turned into a diner, out on its own at the edge of the desert. It's the only stop for miles: inside there's food, a bartender, and a Poké Mart counter, and there's a spot to heal your Pokémon outside. The Desert Trail leads off into the sand, and once you've battled here, your bike can take you to Phenac City.

Two men and a sack

As you arrive, two men hurry out of the diner hauling a big sack between them, and the sack is wriggling. They throw it in their truck and drive off before anyone sees. Remember their faces: you'll meet them again in Phenac City.

Desert Trail

The Desert Trail is the open sand between Orre's towns. Your bike carries you along it from place to place, and as the story goes on, new places along it open up: the track to Team Snagem's old hideout stays buried under blowing sand until Nett has Ein's Data ROM (Part 4), and the road to the Orre Colosseum is closed until much later.

{{tables:ORRE_DESERT}}

Every Shadow Pokémon on the Desert Trail's list is its own trainer's: Trudly's Makuhita and the Mystery Troop's three in Phenac City, and, five years on, Fein's Togetic and the Togepi Hordel gives you. Snag each of the others in its own battle. Once you've snagged one, the Desert Trail's SNAG AGAIN can bring it back.

Phenac City

Phenac City is a town of water in the middle of the desert, built around a big fountain. Its sign calls it "the jewel of the desert". The stadium sits right on top of the water at the top of the town, and the Mayor's house is on the west side. There's a Pokémon Center and a Poké Mart, and the desert begins at the gates.

{{tables:PHENAC_CITY}}

The Super Potions are in a Poké Ball in town. Battle CDs are played at the Pokémon HQ Lab, which you only reach five years on (Part 5 onwards). Battle CD 10 is on a bookshelf in a house now; the others turn up later.

The sack

As you walk into the square, the two men from the Outskirt Stand are dragging their wriggling sack across it. One of them, Folly, spots you staring and tells you to mind your own business.

{{battle:PHENAC_CITY:Shady Guy Folly|In the square, in a story scene.}}

Folly and his partner Trudly drop the sack and run. You undo the rope... and out climbs a girl. She's Rui. The men grabbed her because she saw something she shouldn't have: some of their Pokémon had a dark glow around them, as if their hearts were shut tight. Nobody believes her, but she can see it. She asks to come with you, because the Mayor has to hear about this. Rui joins you, and from now on she can point out Shadow Pokémon in battle.

{{journal:orre_m2b}}

Mayor Es Cade

The Mayor's house is on the west side of town. Mayor Es Cade welcomes you to Phenac. When Rui tells him about Pokémon with a dark aura, he says he has heard whispers of Shadow Pokémon, made to fight and nothing else. He promises to look into it, and suggests you try Phenac Stadium at the top of the town, where strong trainers gather.

An old friend from Snagem

On your way back through town, a man in a Team Snagem jacket blocks the way. It's Wakin, and he knows exactly who you are: the traitor who blew up the hideout. He wants the boss's machine back.

{{battle:PHENAC_CITY:Team Snagem Wakin|In town, after you've met the Mayor · a story scene.}}

Wakin runs, swearing the boss won't let this go. Rui has worked it out now: that machine on your arm can catch other trainers' Pokémon, so you can rescue the Shadow Pokémon! You just need Poké Balls. She thinks the bartender at the Outskirt Stand might have some.

{{journal:orre_m2c}}

Ride back to the Outskirt Stand. The bartender says nobody sells Poké Balls in Orre these days, since there are hardly any wild Pokémon left, but he has a few lying around. He gives you 5 Poké Balls on the house.

Trouble at the Mayor's

Back in Phenac, a man runs up: men in strange outfits have barged into the Mayor's house, and they haven't come out.

{{journal:orre_m2d}}

Inside, a man with a huge afro is dancing. He's Miror B., and he calls the Mayor a dull host before dancing out of the room and leaving his peons to keep you company. They're Folly and Trudly, in new outfits. Folly goes first.

{{battle:PHENAC_CITY:Miror B. Peon Folly|In the Mayor's house.}} {{battle:PHENAC_CITY:Miror B. Peon Trudly|In the Mayor's house, right after Folly.}}

Trudly's Makuhita has the dark aura: it's a Shadow Pokémon. Rui tells you what to do: weaken it, then throw a Poké Ball, and the Snag Machine will catch it.

Phenac's guards

Miror B.'s gang isn't done yet. Three men in coloured outfits, the Mystery Troop, are guarding Phenac's ways out on Miror B.'s orders. Each one has a Shadow Pokémon: Verde has a Bayleef, Rosso a Quilava and Bluno a Croconaw.

{{journal:orre_m3}}

Pick one of the three to battle, and snag his Shadow Pokémon. Once one of them is beaten, all three retreat and the way out of town is clear. The two you didn't battle haven't gone for good: you'll meet them again at Realgam Tower (Part 4).

Phenac Stadium

Now that you can snag, Phenac Stadium's tournament is open to you. Sign up with the receptionist in the stadium's lobby, where there's a healing machine too. In the arena you stand on your side of the field, and each round's opponent walks in to face you. Win four rounds in a row. Before the final, the referee has a challenge for you, a game to win first. Then the stadium's champion, Gym Leader Misty, takes the field.

Lose a round and your Pokémon are healed, you're back in the lobby, and the tournament starts over from round 1.

{{journal:orre_m14}} {{trainers:PHENAC_CITY:Gym}}

The four rounds, in order: Rewn, Buna, Holen, Glais. Beat Glais, the fourth, and you're through to the final: Misty is waiting.

{{battle:PHENAC_CITY:Misty|The final of Phenac Stadium, after its challenge.}}

Misty's team leans on Water, with Castform and Staryu. Beat her and the Phenac Badge is yours, with TM011 Water Pulse.

Around Phenac

A girl by the stadium thinks it's pretty, sitting on the water. A Bird Keeper says Phenac was quiet until the men in uniforms showed up, an old man warns that the desert out east is no place to wander without strong Pokémon, and a mother can hardly sit still because her daughter is out on her first date. A runner does laps round the fountain every morning, and a boy's Poliwrath naps by the Center door.

Pokémon Center and Poké Mart

In the Center, a boy in glasses tells you the nurse heals your team for free, a traveller says riders come through all day because it's the only water for miles, and an old man points out the PC in the corner. In the Mart, a girl says you won't find Poké Balls on sale in Orre and suggests the Outskirt Stand. Once you've beaten one of the Mystery Troop, she's surprised to see them back on the shelves: nobody wanted them for years, and now everyone's asking. And a trainer reminds you that Potions are cheap and a fainted team is not.

With the Phenac Badge in your pocket, your bike can take you to Pyrite Town (see Part 2).
