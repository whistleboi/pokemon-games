The lonely diner at the edge of the desert, where your Orre journey begins. On its TV, the news reports an explosion at Team Snagem's hideout last night. Rider Willie, sure you know your way round a battle, gives you your first one: two on two, with his Zigzagoon. Nobody sells Poké Balls in Orre any more, but the bartender has a few lying around and gives you 5 Poké Balls. The counter also works as a Poké Mart. See Part 1.

Five years later, once the Pre Gym's people are freed, Researcher Hordel is here. He ran away from Cipher and took a Pokémon with him: he hands you a Shadow Togepi to purify, and tells you Team Snagem is back in its old hideout.

Phenac City's Pokémon Center. A boy in glasses tells newcomers that the nurse heals your team for free, and an old man points out the PC for the Pokémon you can't carry.

Phenac's Poké Mart. At first it has no Poké Balls, and a girl here explains why: there are hardly any wild Pokémon in Orre, so nobody sells them. She suggests asking at the Outskirt Stand. Once you've beaten one of the Mystery Troop guarding Phenac, Poké Balls are on the shelves of Orre's Marts again, and she can hardly believe how many people want them now.

The lobby of Phenac Stadium, Orre's first Gym. Once you have the Snag Machine, the receptionist signs you up for the stadium's tournament and sends you out to the arena: four rounds, then Gym Leader Misty's game, then Misty herself. Win the Phenac Badge and TM011 Water Pulse. A healing machine stands in the lobby. See Part 1 and Orre's Colosseums.

After you become Champion, Misty waits in the lobby with a new team, for a battle once a day (Part 10).

The stadium's arena, where the tournament's challengers walk out one by one to face you. Five years later, Cipher takes the stadium over: Snattle's peons wait here to ambush you, and Snattle himself battles you on the stadium floor. Once Phenac is free again, Battle CD 44 lies on the empty floor. See Part 7.

Mayor Es Cade's house. You first meet him here with Rui, who tells him about the Shadow Pokémon; he promises to look into it and sends you to Phenac Stadium. Soon after, Miror B. and his peons raid the house. Miror B. dances off, and his peons Folly and Trudly battle you. Trudly's Makuhita is a Shadow Pokémon, the first you snag. See Part 1.

Five years later, the mayor's secretary keeps everyone out, but she longs for some music. Give her a Music Disc and she dances while you slip upstairs, where an unfinished note warns that Cipher means to take Phenac by wearing its people's faces. The secretary turns out to be Cipher Peon Pellim in disguise, and Pellim and Cipher Peon Fenton battle you. Upstairs you find Battle CDs 12, 27 and 32. See Part 7.

A music lover lives here. Five years later, when the mayor's secretary wants music, he gives you the Music Disc. Battle CD 10 is on his bookshelf.

A quiet house in Phenac. Nobody's home.

The lobby of the Pyrite Colosseum, Orre's second Gym, with a Pokémon Center counter. The Colosseum has no power until the windmill turns again; once it does, and you hold a badge, the receptionist signs you up. Four rounds, then Gym Leader Duking's game, then Duking. Win the Pyrite Badge and TM064 Bulk Up. See Part 2.

After you become Champion, Duking waits in the lobby with a new team, for a battle once a day (Part 10).

The arena upstairs, where the Pyrite Colosseum's tournament is fought. The referee calls each challenger out in turn. See Orre's Colosseums.

Pyrite Town's Poké Mart.

Pyrite's sheriff warns that his town is rough, and that Cipher has been running things from the Under, the old city below. The Jail Key sits on his desk. Take it and free the prisoner, who thanks you with TM018 Thief.

Later, once Dakim has been driven off Mt. Battle, Duking has caught two of Miror B.'s peons, Reath and Ferma, and they're locked in a cell here. Unlock it with the Jail Key: Reath is fast asleep, and you take the Elevator Key off her belt. It works the Pyrite Building's giant lift. See Part 3.

An old couple live here, and they keep their doors shut in these dark days. Five years later, a kid hides behind the bookcase. He saw Cail battle someone with a dark, glowing Pokémon, and his tip starts Colosseum's after-story (Part 11). Once Greevil is beaten, Battle CD 40 turns up on the bookshelf.

Duking's house, where he looks after the town's kids, Silva and Marcia among them. Silva is angry that nobody stands up to Miror B., and soon he goes missing, along with the windmill's gear. Bring the Gear back from the Construction Lot and Duking helps you fit it: the windmill turns, and the Pyrite Colosseum has power again. Later, when you rescue his Plusle from Miror B. in Pyrite Cave, it wants to go with you, and Duking gives you Plusle. Battle CD 22 is on his bookshelf. See Part 2.

A fortune teller sees sand, a long ride, and a Pokémon with a closed heart.

The Pyrite Hotel. Every room is booked.

Agate Village's Pokémon Center. Five years later, an old man here admits he handed out "Pokémon Translators" he got from a Dr. Kaminko, a clue in the mystery of the village's silent Pokémon (Part 11).

Agate Village's Poké Mart.

Agate's Pokémon Day Care. Leave a Pokémon here and it grows while you travel. A Shadow Pokémon left here opens its heart too, a little every 100 steps you walk. See Day Care.

The Day Care's other floor. Nobody's here.

The home of Eagun, Agate's elder, and his wife Beluh: Rui's grandparents. Rui brings you here to tell them about the Shadow Pokémon, but a man bursts in: strangers have pushed into the Relic Cave. Once you've saved Eagun there, Beluh thanks you both, explains that the Relic Stone can open a Shadow Pokémon's heart, and passes on an e-mail about Mt. Battle being attacked. See Part 3.

Five years later, Beluh asks for help when the village's Pokémon go silent, and thanks you with a Lucky Egg (Part 11). After you become Champion and clear Team Rocket's tower, Eagun's Alakazam here can teleport you to the Marine Cave (Part 10).

A woman and her shy daughter live here with their Combusken. Five years later, Combusken has gone silent, with a little machine stuck to it: one of the village's silent Pokémon (Part 11).

Senilor and his Wobbuffet live here. Battle CD 14 is under Wobbuffet. Five years later, Wobbuffet hasn't made a sound since Senilor's son put a "Pokémon Translator" on it: the start of Beluh's side story (Part 11).

In the second half of your journey a Move Tutor is here too. He teaches Body Slam, Double-Edge, Seismic Toss, Mimic, Dream Eater, Thunder Wave, Swords Dance, Nightmare, Self-Destruct, Sky Attack, Substitute and Icy Wind, and sells each as a TM, so you can teach it whenever you like. See TMs and tutors.

Orre's third Gym, led by two Leaders together, Blaine and Erika. They battle you once you hold two badges and have dealt with the trouble in the Relic Cave. The hall has three gardens of numbered flower beds ours. Each bed you step on blooms and adds its number; make a garden's number exactly (10, 18 and 27) and it stays in bloom, but go over and the vines grow back. With all three in bloom, Blaine burns the vines off the door to the Leaders' garden, where a healing machine stands. Win the Agate Badge, TM168 Solar Beam and TM141 Fire Blast. See Part 3.

After you become Champion, Blaine and Erika battle you again once a day with a new team (Part 10).

Gateon Port's Pokémon Center.

Gateon Port's Poké Mart.

A two-floor building in Gateon Port. Battle CD 06 is on the desk. Play it on the Battle CD player at the Pokémon HQ Lab.

A herbalist lives here with a Munchlax that eats everything. If that Munchlax ever raids one of your snack trays at a Poké Spot, come and tell him: he gives you 10 Poké Snacks and a healing herb to make up for it.

A quiet house in Gateon Port. Nobody's home.

Gateon Port's Parts Shop, run by Perr. When the Pokémon HQ Lab needs a Machine Part for the Purify Chamber, Perr has one in the stockroom, but his dad Makan has the key, and he's at Kaminko's House. Once Makan is back he gives you the Machine Part, on the house, and gets Gateon's movable bridges working again. See Part 5.

An old man here rowed over to the shops every morning while Gateon's bridges were stuck, and he's glad Makan fixed them.

An old woman watches the ships from her window. She doesn't like the big grey ones that sail out past the rocks.

Gateon Port's lighthouse is Orre's fifth Gym, kept by Gym Leader Jasmine. Three of the lighthouse's own Pokémon guard the stairs, one on each floor: an Electabuzz, an Electrode and an Ampharos. Get past all three and Jasmine battles you on the roof. Win the Gateon Badge and TM265 Iron Tail. Once the bridges work, Bodybuilder Kilen waits on the roof too. See Part 5.

After you become Champion, Jasmine battles you again on the roof once a day with a new team (Part 10).

Realgam Tower's Pokémon Center, in the square in front of the tower.

Realgam's Poké Mart. Stock up here before the Orre League (Part 9).

The lobby of Realgam Tower, where a hunter, a street performer and a bandana guy wait to battle anyone heading for the tower. One door leads into the Realgam Tower Dome, the inside of the tower, and a lift goes up to the Colosseum in the sky on the roof, locked until you've dealt with Cipher's people inside the tower. See Part 4.

The open-air arena on Realgam Tower's roof. At the end of the story's first half, it's Cipher's Colosseum: its host sends out five challengers, and then Cipher's head, Evice, rises from the floor (Part 4).

Five years later it hosts the Orre League. Gym Leader Birch is here, and once you hold seven badges and have beaten Greevil, he gives you the Realgam Badge and TM193 Weather Ball. With eight badges, the host signs you up for the Elite Four and the Champion (Part 9).

A desk here also runs the Realgam Colosseum: four rounds of four battles, every team at Lv 47, each round paying a cash prize. See Orre's Colosseums.

A building on Realgam's square. Nobody's inside.

A small house on Realgam's square. Nobody's home.

Mr. Fuji, a friend of Eagun's, lives here. The Poké Flute lies on his table for you to take. Once you're Champion, his Alakazam can teleport you to where rare Pokémon gather: the Sky Pillar, Birth Island, the Lake of Rage, and New Island once Mewtwo has fled there. See Part 10.

The lobby of the Orre Colosseum, with a Pokémon Center and a Poké Mart. Once you hold five badges and have beaten Gonzap, the receptionist signs you up for its tournament: three rounds, then Pre Gym Leader Justy. It's a story tournament: there's no badge, but the champion's prize is 10 Poké Balls. See Part 7.

After Greevil, and once you've battled Eagun outside the Pokémon HQ Lab, a desk here runs seven rounds of four battles against the toughest trainers in Orre, each round won paying a TM, prize money and a new title (Part 11).

The arena where the Orre Colosseum's tournament is fought. The referee calls each challenger out in turn. See Orre's Colosseums.

Professor Krane's lab, where the story's second half begins: Cipher carries him off from here, and Lily, who runs the lab with him, asks for your help. Once he's home again, Krane gives you an Eevee. Downstairs is his Purify Chamber, where Shadow Pokémon's hearts open as you travel (see Shadow Pokémon). There's a Pokémon Center counter, a Battle CD player that plays the Battle CDs you find round Orre, and Supertrainer Aferd, who'd like to spar. After Gorigan, Krane gives you the Master Ball to use on Greevil's XD001. See Part 5 and Part 8.

After Greevil, Battle CD 33 turns up on a bookshelf, and Eagun waits outside for a battle (Part 11).

The Under's own Colosseum, with a Pokémon Center and a Poké Mart at its door. Once you hold three badges and have driven Dakim off Mt. Battle, you can enter its tournament: three rounds, then the Under Colosseum's champion, Carben. It's a story tournament: there's no badge, but the champion's prize is 10 Poké Balls. See Part 4.
