At the Musical Theater in Nimbasa City, Pokémon dress up in Props and dance on stage. You pick a Pokémon and a show, dress it up, and it performs with three other dancers. The audience decides who shines most.

Your Prop Case

The first time you walk into the theater, its owner greets you and gives you a Prop Case that a famous star left behind. It already holds six Props: a Top Hat, Blue Barrettes, Square Glasses, a Maraca, the Umber Belt and a Tie. You collect more from fans and from a few people (see below). Then talk to the Musical receptionist to join a show. Any Pokémon in your party can perform (not an Egg).

The four shows

Each show has its own kind and its own audience. Every Prop also has a kind: Cool, Cute, Elegant or Quirky.

Dress Up

Tap a Prop in your Prop Case and it snaps into place on your Pokémon. There are eight spots: the head, the face, the body, the waist, two ears and two arms. Tap a Prop on your Pokémon again to take it off. A count at the bottom shows how many Props of each kind your Pokémon wears.

The show

Your Pokémon and three other dancers perform to the show's own song, each taking a turn in the spotlight. Three times during the show a sum pops up: answer it correctly and the spotlight turns to your Pokémon and the crowd cheers. Get it wrong and another dancer steals the spotlight. ours

Then comes the curtain call, a photo, and the owner's review with your place, from first to fourth.

How the audience scores it

The audience is 200 people. The show's own kind of Prop pleases most of them (80), the opposite kind pleases only 20, and the other two kinds 40 each, with a few more scattered at random. Cool is opposite to Cute, and Elegant is opposite to Quirky. Each group shares its cheers between all the dancers wearing Props of its kind, so wearing Props of the show's kind, when the others don't, wins big. Every spotlight you win adds 15 more points.

Fans, gifts and the rival stylist

  • Fans. After each show, fans wait for you in the lobby. The more points you've earned over all your shows, the more fans come, up to ten. Each fan gives you a Prop you don't have yet, often one of the kind of the show you just played. The fans waiting from one show leave when you play the next, so talk to them first.
  • The owner. He gives you a Toy Cake after your first show, a Crown after your fifth win, and a Winner's Belt after your tenth win.
  • The rival stylist in the theater comments on your last show: the look your Pokémon had, the Prop that stood out, and the kind you left out.
  • Musician Preston on Route 5 gives you an Electric Guitar Prop once you've battled him.
