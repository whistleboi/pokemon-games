Every route, cave and forest is a map you walk, and its wild Pokémon are right there on it with you. Some sit in the tall grass, some wander, and some spot you and come running. Walk into one (or let one walk into you) and you meet it. Which Pokémon live in a place, and at what levels, comes from that place's own game.

Meeting a wild Pokémon

Wild Pokémon move about the map. One that is close enough to see you notices you and chases you; one that reaches the tile beside you jumps in. New ones turn up over time while you walk, so a place never runs dry. Indoors (buildings) none come back.

When you meet one, you choose what to do:

  • MATH throws your selected Poké Ball. You get one math question and 30 seconds to answer. A right answer throws the ball, and a quick one (within 5 seconds) makes the catch much likelier. A wrong answer, or running out of time, and the Pokémon runs away. A shiny Pokémon is caught even on a wrong answer.
  • BATTLE starts a wild battle, with a math question every turn (see Math Battles).

You can't throw balls until the region lets you catch (in Hoenn, that's once Professor Birch gives you your Pokédex), and not without any balls left.

Battle style. In Options, BATTLE STYLE picks Classic (battles on their own screen) or Overworld (the battle is fought right on the map, the two Pokémon side by side). Wild battles follow your choice. The big story battles (Gym Leaders, the Elite Four, the Champion, your rival, a villain team's boss) always use the classic screen.

Wild levels

Each place has its own level, taken from its game's encounter data. A wild Pokémon there comes from 2 levels below that level to 1 above it. Some places use their game's own level range instead (many Sinnoh, Unova and Orange Islands places), and a few single Pokémon have their own range there. Wild levels don't rise to match your team, before or after you become Champion.

Legendary Pokémon are different: before you have every Gym badge of the region, one you meet is 3 levels above the next Leader's best Pokémon. From the last badge on, and after the title, it's level 70.

Surfing

Out on the water with Surf, you meet the place's surfing Pokémon instead of its land ones. Many places have their own surfing table from their game. See Field moves for how you get Surf in each region.

Fishing

Stand beside water with a rod in your Bag and a FISH button appears (tap the water, or face it). Cast, and if something lives there you get a bite and a battle; if not, "not even a nibble".

  • You always fish with your best rod: Super Rod, then Good Rod, then Old Rod.
  • A place's own rod tables come from its game. A better rod reaches bigger fish: what bites is at least level 5 on the Old Rod, 10 on the Good Rod and 15 on the Super Rod.
  • In Unova, each place has its game's own fishing slots with their own levels, whichever rod you hold.

A few of the people who hand out rods: the Fishing Guru in Vermilion City (Old Rod), a fisherman in Dewford Town (Old Rod) and one on Route 209 (Good Rod).

Headbutt

Stand next to a tree in a place that has Headbutt trees and a HEADBUTT button appears. Your Pokémon shakes the tree, and a Pokémon that lives in the trees falls out for a battle. You need the Rocky Helmet in your Bag to do it ours. In Kanto, the sleepy old man in Viridian City gives it to you.

Headbutt trees grow on Kanto's Route 1, Viridian Forest and several more Kanto routes, and on most of Johto's routes (and Ilex Forest).

Rock Smash

Breaking a cracked rock with Rock Smash opens the way. At Hoenn's Mirage spots (Mirage Forest, Mirage Cave, Mirage Mountain and the Mirage Islands) you break the rocks one at a time, and 4 rocks in 10 hide a wild Pokémon from the place's own Rock Smash table.

Honey and Sweet Scent

Use Honey from the Bag on a map, or press H for Sweet Scent, and three wild Pokémon pop out of the grass nearby (out of the water if you're surfing). They walk up and stand round you, then the catch screen opens over the map so you can try for one of the three. The one you catch is gone and the others run off. The White Flute does the same. A kind lady in Veilstone City's store gives you Honey in Sinnoh, and someone in Fallarbor Town does in Hoenn.

Swarms

Some regions have a swarm each day: one place where a certain Pokémon turns up in huge numbers. The place and the Pokémon are picked by the real date, so they stay the same all day. While it's on, the swarm's Pokémon take a big share of that place's encounters (two in five in Sinnoh, Johto and Kanto; half in Hoenn). Some swarms are in the grass, some out on the water while you surf, and some on the end of a fishing line.

A swarm Pokémon you meet in its own place may call two friends over. They walk up and a short battle is fought right on the map: three math problems. Each right answer knocks one out; the last one left, the strongest, you can then catch for sure with your selected ball, or knock out too. A wrong answer, or too slow, and one of them hits your lead Pokémon.

Wild double battles

When someone is walking with you as a partner (in Sinnoh: Cheryl in Eterna Forest, Mira in Wayward Cave, Riley on Iron Island, Buck on Stark Mountain, Marley on Victory Road; in Unova, your rival in the Castelia Sewers), wild Pokémon come two at a time and you and your partner battle both together. After every battle your partner heals your team.

Later regions' Pokémon and regional forms

Kanto, Johto and the Orange Islands stay true to their own Pokémon: a wild Pokémon from a later region (or a later region's form) only shows up there once you've played that region. The Sevii Islands open up Johto's Pokémon early, as in FireRed. The other regions keep their games' mixed lists.

Regional forms (Alolan, Galarian, Hisuian, Paldean) live in their home region. Another region's forms only appear once you've been to that region. Once you've caught every form from one region, those forms start turning up everywhere their usual forms live, about 1 time in 100. Hisuian forms stay in Hisui until then.

Region by region

Kanto and the Sevii Islands

FireRed's Pokémon and levels, the same all day. Headbutt trees on several routes (with the Rocky Helmet), and SoulSilver's nine Kanto swarms from the very start. The Safari Zone has its own four areas.

Johto

HeartGold and SoulSilver's morning, day and night tables for almost every route and cave, including Routes 26 to 28, Tohjo Falls and Mt. Silver. Headbutt trees on most routes. Swarms after the National Pokédex, in the grass, on the water and on the line. The Pokégear radio changes things too: the Pokémon March brings more wild Pokémon out, faster, and the Pokémon Lullaby fewer; Hoenn Sound and Sinnoh Sound bring a place's faraway Pokémon into its grass.

Orange Islands

The fan game's own Pokémon and levels, many places with their own level range, and surfing tables for most islands.

Hoenn

Hordes. In 30 places, 1 wild battle in 10 is a horde: five wild Pokémon at once, your one Pokémon against all of them (as in Omega Ruby and Alpha Sapphire). Hordes are often lower in level than the single Pokémon there.

The DexNav. Your rival gives you the DexNav on Route 101. With it, now and then (about once every thirty steps) a hidden Pokémon shows itself near you: a tail flicking in the grass, on a cave floor or in deep sand, or a ripple out on the water while you surf. Creep onto its spot and you meet it, but it slips away after 18 steps. These hidden Pokémon are each place's own from Omega Ruby and Alpha Sapphire. The ones from Generations 1 to 3 appear once Groudon or Kyogre is calmed at the Cave of Origin; later ones once you're Hoenn's Champion.

Plus Emerald's outbreaks after the Hall of Fame, Rock Smash Pokémon at the Mirage spots, and the Safari Zone.

Sinnoh

Day and night tables (many places have Pokémon that only come out at night), and Platinum's swarms after the National Pokédex.

The Poké Radar. Roseanne in the lab at Sandgem Town gives you the Poké Radar once you have the National Pokédex. Charge it by walking 50 steps, then use it standing in tall grass: four patches of grass nearby shake. Step into one and a wild Pokémon jumps out. Afterwards the grass shakes again by itself. Step into a patch and meet the same kind again and your chain grows: the same kind gets likelier in the patches, and so does a shiny patch (about 1 in 200 at a chain of 40). Step anywhere else, or leave, and the chain breaks. With the National Pokédex the radar also finds Platinum's radar-only Pokémon.

Also the Great Marsh.

Unova

Each Unova place uses Black 2 and White 2's own slots, slot by slot, each with its own level range. Both versions' Pokémon are in every table, whichever version you play.

Seasons. The season changes every month, and some places (Route 6, Route 7, Route 20) have a different table for each season.

Dark grass. Patches of darker grass hold stronger Pokémon from their own slots.

Phenomena. Every so often (after about 20 quiet steps) something happens near you:

  • Rustling grass: a patch of grass shakes. Step in and its own Pokémon (such as Audino) jumps out.
  • Rippling water: a spot of water ripples. Surf onto it for its Pokémon, or fish in it for its own fishing Pokémon.
  • Dust clouds (in caves such as the Relic Passage and Chargestone Cave): half the time a Shard is buried in the cloud, else its Pokémon bursts out.
  • Flying shadows (on the Driftveil Drawbridge): a bird's shadow crosses the bridge. Step on it and half the time a Wing feather drifts down, else the bird swoops in.

Walk well away, or change floors, and it stops.

Hidden Grottoes. A gap in the trees leads to a small clearing (on Route 5, Route 6, Route 7, at Floccesy Ranch and in Lostlorn Forest). Each holds one thing: a Pokémon with its Hidden Ability, an item on the ground, or a buried item. Once you've taken it (or met the Pokémon) it's empty. After that, every 256 steps you walk in Unova it has a 1 in 20 chance to fill again: a Pokémon 1 time in 5, an item 2 in 5, a buried item 2 in 5. Your first visit to Route 5's grotto always finds a Minccino. See Hidden Grottoes for more.
