Every battle in the game is a turn-by-turn Pokémon battle with one twist: each time you pick a move, a math question pops up, and your answer decides how well that move goes. A right answer makes the move work at full strength. A quick right answer can even give it a bonus. A wrong answer still uses the move, just more weakly. ours

A turn, step by step

A battle opens with four buttons: FIGHT, BAG, POKéMON and RUN.

  1. FIGHT shows your Pokémon's four moves. Each button shows the move's type, its power and its accuracy (or "Sure hit"), its PP, and a mark when it's super effective or not very effective on the foe. A move with no PP left can't be picked.
  2. Pick a move and a math question appears with three answers to choose from. There's no countdown on the battle screen: take the time you need.
  3. Then the turn plays out. You and the foe act in order of move priority, then Speed, as in the main games.

What your answer does

Your answer and your move
Your answerWhat happens
RightThe move works as normal. You hear a cheer and the battle goes on.
Right, in under 2 secondsA speedy bonus, picked at random: the move hits with its top power, it lands a critical hit, or (if the foe hasn't moved yet this turn) a 30% chance the foe flinches.
WrongThe game kindly shows the right answer. Your move still goes, but weaker: a lower damage roll, and never more than 49% of the foe's full HP in one move.

Every move asks a question, status moves too. Switching Pokémon or using an item from the Bag doesn't.

How hard the math is

The questions come from eight levels, named after Poké Balls. In Options > MATH you can pick a level, or leave it on AUTO. On AUTO the math grows with the total number of badges you hold, across every region.

Two battle styles

In Options > GAMEPLAY, Battle style picks how battles look. The rules and the math are the same either way.

  • Classic: battles open on their own battle screen, with your Pokémon and the foe facing each other.
  • Overworld: on a walked map, a trainer's words show in the map's own text box and the battle is fought right there on the map. If the spot is cramped, you and the foe first walk over to open ground nearby, and you walk back afterwards. Both Pokémon stand on the map with small HP bars over them, and each attack flies across as a little orb in the colour of its type. Wild Pokémon you bump into on a walked map are fought there too. The buttons and math questions are the classic ones. ours
Always on the battle screen. The big story battles never move onto the map, whatever the style: Gym Leaders, your rival, a villain team's boss, the Elite Four, the Champion, and any other boss battle. Wild battles beside a walking partner stay on the battle screen too.

Bigger battles

Double battles

Some trainers battle two-on-two. Each of your two Pokémon picks its own action in turn, and each move it uses gets its own math question, so a double battle asks two questions a turn. A move that can hit more than one Pokémon (Earthquake, Surf, Rock Slide and the like) hits all it can reach at three-quarters power; Earthquake and Surf hit your partner too. Moves made for partners (Helping Hand, Follow Me, After You and others) work here. A fainted Pokémon is replaced at the end of the turn. If you have only one Pokémon left that can battle, you battle with one.

Black 2 and White 2's Triple Battles are played here as double battles.

Tag battles

In a few story moments someone battles beside you: their Pokémon fill your second spot and choose their own moves. Sometimes two trainers stand on the other side, each with their own Pokémon. You lose when your own Pokémon are all out, and only your Pokémon earn Exp.

Two wild Pokémon at once

While a walking partner travels with you (in Sinnoh and Unova), wild Pokémon come two at a time, and you and your partner face them together. You can throw a Poké Ball only when one of them is left standing. After every battle your partner heals your team.

Hordes

In many of Hoenn's grassy and rocky places, about one wild meeting in ten is a horde: five wild Pokémon at once against your one. Moves that hit all foes hit all five. You can throw a Poké Ball only when one is left, and every one you knock out gives Exp.

Region by region

Single battles are everywhere. Where the bigger battles turn up:

Kanto and the Sevii Islands

Pairs of trainers battle you two-on-two on Route 8, Route 12, Route 14, Route 15, Route 16, Route 19, Route 21 and in Victory Road. On the Sevii Islands they wait on Kindle Road, Bond Bridge, Water Path, Outcast Island, Canyon Entrance and Sevault Canyon. The Trainer Tower has Double and Mixed climbs (see Battle Facilities).

Johto

Twins and other pairs battle two-on-two in Azalea Town, on Route 37, Route 47, the S.S. Aqua and in Dragon's Den. In the Team Rocket HQ, Lance battles beside you against Executive Ariana and a Rocket Grunt.

Orange Islands

Luana's Gym on Kumquat Island is a double battle.

Hoenn

Tate and Liza's Gym in Mossdeep City is a double battle. Pairs also battle two-on-two on Route 107 and Route 117, in the Trick House, Meteor Falls, the Abandoned Ship and on the S.S. Tidal, and the interviewers Gabby and Ty wait on Route 111 and Route 118. At the Space Center, Steven battles beside you against the villain team's leader and admin. Hordes appear in many places, from Route 101 to Victory Road.

Sinnoh

Pairs battle two-on-two on Route 204, Route 209, Route 210, in the Lost Tower and Victory Road. At Valor Lakefront, two trainers at a time take you on in double battles. In Veilstone City, Dawn battles beside you against two Galactic Grunts. Walking partners join you in Eterna Forest (Cheryl), Wayward Cave (Mira), on Iron Island (Riley) and in Victory Road (Marley).

Unova

Pairs and Triple Battle trainers (played as doubles) wait on Route 4, Route 5, Route 7, Route 16, Route 20, in Driftveil City and Chargestone Cave. On the Plasma Frigate, Cheren or your rival battles beside you against Team Plasma. Your rival walks with you through the Castelia Sewers as a walking partner. The Battle Subway's Double and Multi Trains and the Pokémon World Tournament's double battles are in Battle Facilities.

Winning, losing and Exp.

  • Every Pokémon that earns Exp. also gets the foe's full effort points.
  • With Exp Share on (Options), the whole party earns Exp. from a win. EXP Mode (Options, Difficulty) gives 30% more Exp. from every battle.
  • A Pokémon at the level cap earns no more Exp. until you beat the next Gym: see Level Cap.
  • If all your Pokémon faint, you hurry back to the last Pokémon Center you used in that region (or home, if you haven't used one yet), and your team is healed. Battles in the battle facilities are the exception: you're healed and sent back to the lobby instead.
  • Mega Evolution and Primal Reversion add a MEGA or PRIMAL button to the battle menu: see Mega Evolution.
