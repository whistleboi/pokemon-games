Math is at the heart of this game. Every move in battle asks a question, every Poké Ball you throw at a wild Pokémon asks one, and math turns up in contests, Gyms, games and puzzles all over the world. One question maker makes almost all of them, so the questions are always at your level, wherever you are. ours

Picking your level

There are eight math levels, each named after a Poké Ball. You choose one when you start a new game, and you can change it any time in Options > MATH. While you look, the bottom of that screen shows an example question at your level.

Or leave it on AUTO: then the math grows with you. AUTO counts all the badges you hold, in every region put together, and moves you up a level as the total grows. It starts at Great Ball, so Poké Ball, the very easiest level, is only used if you pick it yourself.

Kinds of questions

A question can be asked in five ways. Each level mixes them in its own amounts:

The five kinds of question
KindLooks likeWhat you do
Solve3 + 4 = ?Pick the answer. The wrong choices are close to it (a few more or a few less, or ten off for bigger answers), and for times tables, one row off in the table.
Missing number3 + ? = 7  or  ? + 4 = 7Pick the number that's missing, from the front or the middle.
Equation7 = ?Pick the sum that makes 7. The wrong sums look a lot like it, but don't come to 7.
Compare8 ? 4Pick the sign that fits: <, = or > (always in that order). About one time in four the two sides are equal.
Compare and fill8 > ?  or  ? < 8Three numbers to choose from: one smaller than 8, 8 itself and one bigger. Only one makes the sign true.

In a comparison, a side can be a sum of its own, in brackets: (5 + 8) ? 13. How often that happens grows with the levels.

In battle

Pick a move and its question appears, with four answers to choose from (three for a comparison). There's no countdown in battle: take your time.

  • Right: the move works as normal.
  • Right, in under 2 seconds: a speedy bonus. The move hits with top power, lands a critical hit, or may make the foe flinch.
  • Wrong: the game shows you the right answer, and your move still goes, only weaker.

In a double battle each of your Pokémon gets its own question. The whole story is on Math Battles.

Catching

When you choose MATH to catch a wild Pokémon, you get one question with three answers and 30 seconds to answer. A right answer throws your ball. Answer right within 5 seconds and the throw counts as if the Pokémon were down to 1 HP, which makes the catch much likelier. A wrong answer, or running out of time, and the Pokémon gets away (a shiny one is caught even on a wrong answer). The same 30 seconds go for the short swarm battles fought on the map. See Wild Pokémon.

The Answer timer

In Options > MATH, the Answer timer sets how long these countdowns last: Normal, 50% more time, or Off (no countdown at all). The same setting stretches the time you get to say yes to "Throw a ball?" when a wild Pokémon is weak in battle.

Math Stats

The game keeps count of every question you answer. Open STATS from your MENU to see how many you've answered, how many you got right, your best streak in a row and your quick answers, with how well you do at adding, taking away, times and sharing, and at each level. Some feats in the Journal count right answers in a row too.
