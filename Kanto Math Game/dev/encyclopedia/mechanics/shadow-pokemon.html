In Orre, Cipher turns Pokémon into Shadow Pokémon: Pokémon whose hearts have been shut tight so that they do nothing but fight. Hardly any wild Pokémon live in Orre, so most of the Pokémon you add to your team there are Shadow Pokémon that you take from the trainers who use them, with the Snag Machine on your arm. Once snagged, a Shadow Pokémon fights for you, and the job is to open its heart again, so it can be purified and become an ordinary Pokémon. This is how Pokémon Colosseum and XD: Gale of Darkness play, and Orre's fan game adds Shadow Pokémon of its own.

Seeing them

A Shadow Pokémon looks like any other until someone can see its dark aura. In Orre that's Rui: once she travels with you (from Phenac City), Shadow Pokémon are marked in battle. Each place's walkthrough tables list the Shadow Pokémon you can snag there and which trainer has each one.

Snagging

  • With the Snag Machine, you can throw a Poké Ball from the Bag at another trainer's Shadow Pokémon in the middle of a battle. The machine's arm glows and the Ball flies. Weaken the Pokémon first, as you would a wild one.
  • Each Shadow Pokémon has its own snag chance, taken from the Colosseum and XD games. A Master Ball always works.
  • Throw a Ball at a trainer's ordinary Pokémon and the trainer blocks it ("Don't be a thief!"). The Ball isn't used up.
  • A snagged Pokémon joins your party, or goes to the PC if your party is full.
  • In a double battle, the Ball goes to the Shadow Pokémon on the other side.

Missed one?

Many of Orre's places have a SNAG SHADOW POKÉMON option: a Cipher trainer sends out the next Shadow Pokémon from that place that you haven't snagged yet. Once every one there is snagged, the option becomes SNAG AGAIN ours: a straggler sends out another of that place's Shadow Pokémon, so you can keep one as a Shadow Pokémon and purify another.

Later in the story there are more second chances. Once you have Miror B.'s radar, every XD Shadow Pokémon you beat without snagging goes to Miror B., who dances at one of the Poké Spots each day: the radar tells you which. Colosseum's missed Shadow Pokémon wait in the Snagem Hideout instead, after the five years have passed. See Part 11.

The Heart Gauge

A snagged Shadow Pokémon has a Heart Gauge of five bars, full when you snag it. As the gauge goes down, the heart opens.

  • Battles. Every battle it takes part in opens it a little.
  • Walking. Walking with it in your party opens it a little every 64 steps.
  • Calling it. Calming it down when it's in Hyper Mode (below) opens it a little too.

The gauge also hides its moves. A Shadow Pokémon knows Shadow Rush, and its own moves show as "????". Each time a bar empties it remembers one of them, and by the last bar it remembers them all. It still gains Exp. and levels up as it battles.

When the gauge is empty, its heart is open and it's ready to be purified.

Shadow moves

Shadow moves are a type of their own. They're twice as strong against ordinary Pokémon and half as strong against other Shadow Pokémon. Cipher's Shadow Pokémon use them against you, so a Shadow Pokémon of your own takes less damage from them.

Hyper Mode and CALL

In battle, a Shadow Pokémon may lose control and go into Hyper Mode. The fuller its Heart Gauge, the more likely that is. In Hyper Mode, half the time it ignores the move you choose and uses Shadow Rush instead.

While your Shadow Pokémon is out in a trainer battle, the RUN button becomes CALL. Calling to a Pokémon in Hyper Mode calms it down, and opens its heart a little. Calling to one that isn't just makes it pay attention. Calling takes your turn. Hyper Mode also ends when the battle is won.

Purifying

Purifying turns a Shadow Pokémon back into an ordinary one of its species. It keeps its level, gets its own moves back, loses Shadow Rush, and it's fond of you from the start. Purified at the Relic Stone or with a Time Flute, a Pokémon that's already past the level where it would evolve evolves straight away.

The Purify Chamber

The chamber on the lab's lower floor has nine sets. Each set holds one Shadow Pokémon in its middle and up to four of your other Pokémon in a ring round it. Pokémon in the chamber leave your party and PC while they're in it.

  • Every 161 steps you walk, the Shadow Pokémon in each set opens its heart a little. The more Pokémon in its ring, the faster: a full ring of four opens it nearly ten times as fast as a ring of one.
  • How well the ring works together, its flow, speeds it up even more. Go round the ring in order: for each Pokémon, check its best type against the next one. Super effective adds to the flow; not very effective (or no effect) takes from it. A Normal type counts as super effective against another Normal type.
  • A full ring where every one of the four is super effective against the next is at Max Tempo, and opens the heart twice as fast as a ring with no flow.
  • When a heart opens, a message from the lab tells you. Go back and purify it there.
XD001. Greevil's Shadow Lugia, XD001, has a heart shut so tight that neither the Relic Stone nor a Time Flute can reach it. The chamber takes it only when all nine sets are at Max Tempo at once. Then its heart opens straight away, and once purified, Lugia can also learn Feather Dance and Psycho Boost.

Ho-Oh

Snag every Shadow Pokémon in Orre and purify every kind at least once, and Ho-Oh appears at the Relic Stone in the Relic Forest. You may keep some Shadow Pokémon as they are: each kind only needs to have been purified once. See Part 11.
