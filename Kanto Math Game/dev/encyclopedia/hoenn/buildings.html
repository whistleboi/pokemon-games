Your new house in Littleroot Town, where the game begins on moving day. Talk to Mom any time and she heals your team; she keeps your prize money safe too (Part 1). After you beat your dad at the Petalburg Gym, Mom gives you an Amulet Coin.

Later visits matter as well. After you become Champion, Mom's news about a meteor heading for Hoenn starts the Delta Episode (Part 18). Once that's over, she finds an odd stone in the yard after a meteor shower: the Latiasite in Omega Ruby, or the Latiosite in Alpha Sapphire.

Professor Birch's family home, next door to yours. Mrs. Birch welcomes you as the new neighbour and sends you upstairs, where your rival, the Professor's child, introduces themselves before rushing off to help their dad on Route 101 (Part 1).

Professor Birch's Lab. The Professor is hardly ever in: he'd rather be out in the tall grass. After you've battled your rival on Route 103, come back here: he gives you a Pokédex, and your rival adds ten Poké Balls (Part 2).

Your dad Norman's Gym. He won't battle you until you hold four Hoenn badges, so come back after Lavaridge Town. Inside is a row of small rooms, each with an Ace Trainer. A room's doors stay shut until you beat its trainer, and most rooms have two doors onward, so you choose your own path to Norman at the back.

Norman uses Normal Pokémon, and Fighting moves are your best bet. Win the Balance Badge, TM257 Retaliate and five Poké Balls. The badge lets you Surf on the map, and Wally's father gives you HM Surf next door (Part 11). See Gyms.

Wally's family home. Wally is frail, so his parents are sending him to Verdanturf, where the air is cleaner (Part 3). Come back after you win the Balance Badge and his father gives you HM Surf (Part 11).

An old traveller here remembers roaming all of Hoenn, and a girl dreams of her own Pokémon adventure.

A neighbour here talks about Norman moving his family to Petalburg, and a trainer who once lost to him says he's far too strong.

The Pretty Petal Flower Shop on the north part of Route 104, run by three sisters who love Berries. The eldest explains how Berries grow, the middle sister gives you the Wailmer Pail for watering them, and the youngest gives you one Berry a day (Part 3).

Roxanne's Gym, usually your first in Hoenn. Three trainers stand in the way, and the Gym Guide tips you off that Water and Grass moves work well against Roxanne's Rock Pokémon.

Win the Stone Badge, TM036 Rock Tomb and five Poké Balls; the Hoenn Map comes with the badge. The Stone Badge lets your Pokémon use Cut on the map (Part 4). See Gyms.

The Trainers' School. The teacher gives you a Quick Claw, and the blackboard and students go over status problems, confusion and held Berries (Part 4).

The Devon Corporation, Hoenn's big company, which makes items, medicine, Poké Balls and the PokéNav. On 2F the Devon Fossil Lab brings fossils back to life. The President, Mr. Stone, has his office on 3F. After you win back the stolen Devon Parts, he asks you to deliver a Letter to his son Steven in Dewford and the parts to Captain Stern in Slateport (Part 4).

If you ever find an Intriguing Stone, Mr. Stone trades you a Pidgeotite for it. During the Delta Episode, grunts of Team Magma (Omega Ruby) or Team Aqua (Alpha Sapphire) turn up outside (Part 18).

A family house. Downstairs, a man talks about Abilities; upstairs, a boy and his mother chat.

Trader Ken lives here. Bring him a Slakoth and he trades you his Makuhita, Makit, who holds an X Attack. See In-game trades.

The Cutter's house. He can tell you're a skilled trainer and gives you HM Cut; your Pokémon can use it on the map once you hold the Stone Badge (Part 4).

A mother here praises the Trainers' School, and a fan of Roxanne says she knows everything about Pokémon.

Three floors of flats where Devon's workers live. Everyone here talks about the company and its President.

Home of Pekachu the Pikachu, whose owner is very proud of that nickname.

The Tunneler's Rest House by Rusturf Tunnel. The workers explain why they stopped digging with machines, and that one man is digging through to Verdanturf by hand (Part 4).

Brawly's Gym is pitch dark. Each of his three trainers you beat makes it a little brighter. Brawly only takes challengers who already hold a Hoenn badge, and he uses Fighting Pokémon, so Flying and Psychic moves work well.

Win the Knuckle Badge, TM064 Bulk Up, three Rare Candies and five Poké Balls; an Old Rod comes with the badge (Part 5).

An island couple and their Zigzagoon, happy with their slow island life.

Dewford Hall, where everyone talks about what's in fashion. A collector says Sludge Bomb is the trendiest move of all, and once you hold the Balance Badge he gives you TM148 Sludge Bomb (Part 5).

The house by the pier. A stylish Ace Trainer shows off a Silk Scarf, sees you like it and gives it to you; it powers up Normal moves (Part 5).

Mr. Sea's Seashore House on the beach. He loves a red-hot battle: beat all five trainers inside and he cools you down with six bottles of Soda Pop. After that he sells Soda Pop whenever you like (Part 6).

The Name Rater rates your Pokémon's nicknames, and changes them if you like.

The Pokémon Fan Club. Its chairman loves Pokémon that love their trainers, and gives you five Contest scarves: Red, Blue, Pink, Green and Yellow. The members share tips on keeping your Pokémon happy (Part 6).

Slateport's harbour. Mr. Briney's boat runs back to Dewford from here. After you become Champion the S.S. Tidal sails from here to the Battle Frontier, and with an Eon Ticket a ship takes you to Southern Island (Part 6, Part 20).

Captain Stern's Shipyard. You come here to deliver the Devon Parts, but Stern has gone to the Oceanic Museum, so his assistant Dock sends you on there (Part 6). Much later, bring Stern the Scanner from the Abandoned Ship and he trades you a DeepSeaTooth or a DeepSeaScale, your choice.

A man here explains natures with his Hasty Pokémon, and a girl recommends the Market for battle items.

The south gate of the Seaside Cycling Road. You need a Bicycle, from Rydel in Mauville, to ride up onto it (Part 7).

The north gate of the Seaside Cycling Road, at the Mauville end.

Wattson's Gym is a maze of electric barriers. Step on a switch and every barrier flips: open ones close and closed ones open, so plan your route. Wattson only takes challengers with two Hoenn badges, and you have to battle Wally outside first. Four trainers wait inside.

Wattson uses Electric Pokémon, and Ground moves work best. Win the Dynamo Badge, TM048 Volt Switch and five Poké Balls. The badge lets your Pokémon use Rock Smash on the map, which opens Route 111 (Part 7).

Rydel's Cycles. Rydel is delighted you came all the way from Littleroot and gives you a Bicycle for free, as long as you tell people about his shop. Handbooks at the back explain how to ride (Part 7).

Home of the Rock Smash Dude, who'd like more respect. He explains how Rock Smash breaks cracked rocks.

The Mauville Game Corner has four rows of machines: walk up to one to play Voltorb Flip, or talk to the clerk for the Game Corner menu. You need a Coin Case and Coins to play, and the counter swaps your Coins for prizes. The regulars share tips about the slots (Part 7). See Game Corners.

The mirror shop, full of mirrors of every shape. One of them is said to show the true form of the Pokémon that command the wind, the thunder and the land. Bring Tornadus, Thundurus or Landorus in your party and the owner gives you the Reveal Glass (Part 7).

Mauville Hills, a smart block of flats. TM060 U-turn has been left out on the balcony. Two move tutors live here: one teaches the Pledge moves, and the other teaches Frenzy Plant, Blast Burn and Hydro Cannon to a fully evolved first partner that loves its trainer. Each sells its move as a TM, so it stays unlocked for good ours (Part 7). See TMs and tutors.

The Pokémon Day Care on Route 117. The Day-Care Man waits outside and his wife looks after Pokémon inside: leave one with her and it grows while you travel, and two that get along may find an Egg (Part 8). See Day Care.

Wally now lives here with his uncle, and he's training hard with his Ralts. Wanda and her family are here too; her boyfriend is the man who dug Rusturf Tunnel by hand (Part 8).

A man here tells you how much your Pokémon like you.

Two people here talk about the Pokémon League in Ever Grande City.

A kind old lady's little house on Route 111. Talk to her and she lets your Pokémon rest, healing them completely (Part 8).

The Glass Workshop on Route 113 turns Mt. Chimney's ash into glass. Its counter sells glass flutes: Blue, Yellow, Red, Black and White (Part 8).

Fallarbor's Pokémon Center has a famous visitor: Lanette, who looks after the Pokémon Storage System you use on the PC.

Professor Cozmo studies meteorites. When you arrive, his wife tells you strange people have taken him to Meteor Falls (Part 8). After you rescue him there, he gives you a stone he found that isn't a meteorite at all: the Galladite (Part 9).

The Move Reminder is out of work: here every Pokémon keeps every move it has learned, and you pick its four in the Summary ours.

The Fossil Maniac's house. His little brother gives you TM055 Dig (Part 9).

The tunnel behind the Fossil Maniac's house, where he digs for fossils. After you become Champion, it leads on to the Desert Underpass (Part 20).

Lanette's house. She looks after the Pokémon Storage System and has made the Boxes hold far more Pokémon than before.

Lavaridge's Pokémon Center opens out the back onto the town's hot spring, where a soak refreshes your Pokémon. The Center also sells Moomoo Milk (Part 10).

Flannery's Gym is full of steam and hidden trainers, over two floors. Sand pits drop you to B1F, and vents down there shoot you back up, so find the right way to Flannery. She only takes challengers with three Hoenn badges, and Water Pokémon are a good choice.

Win the Heat Badge, TM157 Overheat, three Rare Candies, five Poké Balls and a Root Fossil for the Devon Fossil Lab. The badge lets your Pokémon use Strength on the map, and the Go-Goggles come with it, so you can cross Route 111's desert (Part 10).

The Herb Shop sells bitter herbal medicine: Energy Powder, Energy Root, Heal Powder and Revival Herb. The old man here gives you Charcoal, which powers up Fire moves (Part 10).

An old man whose wife warms Eggs in the hot spring, and a boy who talks like the town's old folks.

Winona's Gym is full of turnstile gates: push an arm and the gate swings round, so work out which way to turn each one. An invisible Kecleon blocks the door at first; use the Devon Scope from Route 120 to make it flee. Winona only takes challengers with five Hoenn badges, and five trainers wait inside.

Winona uses Flying Pokémon; Electric, Ice and Rock moves work well. Win the Feather Badge, TM268 Roost, three Rare Candies and five Poké Balls. The badge lets you Fly on the map (Part 12).

A little rest stop on Route 119, home to a pair of Wingull.

A treetop house where a little girl wants a Spinda, which lives on Route 113. Trade her one and you get her Skitty, holding a Magost Berry (Part 12). See In-game trades.

An old woman plays a coin game to stay awake. Win three in a row and she gives you TM272 Hidden Power; lose and you can just try again (Part 12).

A stone hunter here once met a trainer called Steven, and a boy admires your Pokédex.

A breeder's Wingull carries letters between here and Mossdeep City. After you've met its friend in Mossdeep and sent a letter, the Wingull flies home and the breeder gives you a Mental Herb it found on the way (Part 12, Part 14).

A family here says Fortree's treehouses make it the best town for living with Pokémon.

This used to be a furniture shop. Now it's the Secret Base Guild, still being set up, and its leader Aarune would like a battle (Part 12).

The gatehouse into Hoenn's Safari Zone, on Route 121. Each visit you get 30 Safari Balls and 500 steps, and inside you can only catch with Safari Balls (Part 13). See Safari Game.

A rest house inside the Safari Zone. The visitors share catching tips: get close, and use Pokéblocks and the feeders.

The Lilycove Department Store: five floors and a rooftop of shopping, Hoenn's biggest. The Lottery Corner on 1F lets you draw once a day for a prize. 2F sells travel and trainer goods, 3F vitamins and battle items, 4F TMs, 5F Pokéblocks and Contest goods, and the rooftop stand sells drinks (Part 13). See Department Stores.

The Lilycove Museum shows paintings on its ground floor. The curator keeps the hall upstairs for portraits of Contest champions: it opens once one of your Pokémon wins a Master Rank Contest, and each Master Rank win hangs a portrait of your Pokémon. Win all five kinds and the curator gives you a Glass Ornament (Part 13). See Contests.

The Cove Lily Motel. Hardly anyone stays since the team moved into the cove, but a group called Game Freak has booked the upper floor. Their game designer gives you a Diploma once your Hoenn Pokédex is complete. Scott is staying here too, watching the Contests (Part 13).

The Pokéblock Master, a mother of four, teaches you how Pokéblocks raise a Pokémon's Contest condition, one colour for each, and gives you one Pokéblock of each colour (Part 13). See Contests.

The Move Deleter used to help Pokémon forget moves, but he's forgotten how. Your Pokémon keep every move anyway ours.

The Pokémon Trainer Fan Club, whose members argue every day about who the strongest trainer is.

A kind man and his Kecleon.

A sleepy man here mumbles that sleep is the best medicine and hands you TM085 Rest without quite waking up.

Two people here wonder about the mysteries at the bottom of the sea, and a trench between Mossdeep and Sootopolis.

The lobby of Lilycove's Contest Hall, where Pokémon Contests are held. Sign up at the counter and collect prizes at the prize desk. Fans crowd round the Berry Blenders making Pokéblocks (Part 13). See Contests.

The Contest stages, one for each kind of Contest, with an MC and a judge at each. The entrants and the crowd swap tips: match your moves to the kind of Contest. See Contests.

Lilycove's harbour. Nothing sails from here until you become Champion; then the S.S. Tidal runs (Part 20).

The Berry Master's house on Route 123. Once a day he gives you two Berries, and his wife gives you one more of her own (Part 13).

The Treasure Hunter collects Shards. Bring him one and he trades you an evolution stone: a Fire Stone for a red Shard, a Thunder Stone for yellow, a Water Stone for blue and a Leaf Stone for green. Route 124 has one of each colour (Part 14).

A man here can tell which Pokéblocks your first Pokémon likes just by looking at it.

A girl here writes to a friend in Fortree City. Talk to her Wingull and it flies off with a letter; visit the breeder in Fortree afterwards for a Mental Herb (Part 14).

A fisherman here gives you the Super Rod, which hooks Pokémon right off the seafloor (Part 14).

Steven's house, full of his polished rare stones. He has left the Metagrossite on his table for you. Come back after you beat Tate and Liza and he gives you HM Dive, with a hint about the cavern under the sea. After you become Champion, a Poké Ball on his table holds a Beldum for you (Part 14).

A little explorer here loves finding hidden spots in the bushes and having battles there.

The Hex Maniac sisters' house. One sister reads the past in your Gym Badges, and once a day she quizzes you on them for a reward (Part 14).

Tate and Liza's Gym is a puzzle of coloured switches and arrow blocks: step on a switch and every block of that colour turns, so change the arrows to clear a path. Some tiles whisk you off to another part of the Gym. Six trainers wait inside, and the twins only take challengers with six Hoenn badges.

Tate and Liza use Psychic Pokémon and battle you together. Win the Mind Badge, TM129 Calm Mind and five Poké Balls. The badge lets your Pokémon Dive on the map, once Steven gives you the HM (Part 14).

Pacifidlog's Pokémon Center has a move tutor: a man who teaches Explosion, and sells it as a TM too ours (Part 16). See TMs and tutors.

An old storyteller here talks about three Pokémon of rock, ice and steel, each hidden in a cave, and his grandchild repeats a riddle about six dots and three doors. It's a clue for the Regi hunt (Part 19).

Once a week, a man here checks how your first Pokémon feels about you. If it likes you he gives you a TM of Return; if not, Frustration (Part 16).

A girl here wants a Bagon that someone else caught. Trade her one and you get her Horsea (Part 16). See In-game trades.

People here have seen a Pokémon flying high over Hoenn.

A scholar who studies Mirage Island gives you TM054 Psyshock for crossing all that sea. His friend looks for the island every day (Part 16).

An old man who has trained in Sootopolis for thirty years gives you TM058 Brick Break (Part 16).

A woman here remembers the two Orbs that once sat together on Mt. Pyre's summit.

Two fans of the Gym Leader, who has fans from all over.

A ruin hunter here has heard about an ancient ruin in the sea nearby.

A researcher here thinks Sootopolis and Mt. Pyre are connected somehow.

A girl who never gets visitors is so happy to see you that she gives you a big Wailmer Doll (Part 16).

A historian here explains how a volcano's crater filled with rainwater to become the city.

Two fans want to see Pokémon you don't find in Sootopolis. Show one a Barboach and the other a Shroomish in your party, and each gives you a Max Elixir (Part 16).

A retired trainer who loves hearing about trainers like you.

Wallace's Gym, Hoenn's eighth and last. Its floor is ice: step on every tile in a section to open the stairs to the next. Each tile cracks when you step on it, and stepping on a cracked tile again drops you to B1F, where all the Gym's trainers wait. Clear three sections on the way up to Wallace. He returns to the Gym after the crisis at the Cave of Origin.

Wallace uses Water Pokémon; Electric and Grass moves are your best choices, but Electric moves can't touch his Whiscash. Win the Rain Badge, HM Waterfall, a Claw Fossil, five Poké Balls and the Eon Ticket for the ship to Southern Island. With eight badges, the road to the League is open (Part 16).

The Pokémon Center below Victory Road. Scott is here to cheer you on before the League (Part 17).

The Pokémon League's front hall, past Victory Road. Only trainers with all eight Hoenn badges may go on. Heal at the counter first: the Elite Four battle you one after another (Part 17).

The first room. Sidney uses Dark Pokémon; Fighting, Bug and Fairy moves work well. The far door opens once you've won (Part 17).

The second room. Phoebe trained on Mt. Pyre and uses Ghost Pokémon; Ghost and Dark moves are good choices (Part 17).

The third room. Glacia uses Ice Pokémon; bring Fire, Fighting, Rock or Steel moves (Part 17).

The fourth room. Drake uses Dragon Pokémon; Ice, Dragon and Fairy moves work well (Part 17).

The Champion's room, where Steven waits with his Rock and Steel team and a Mega Metagross. Win and Professor Birch rushes in, then the door to the Hall of Fame opens (Part 17).

The Hall of Fame. Steven leads you in and the team that made you Champion is recorded with every Champion before you (Part 17).

The Battle Tower, one of the Battle Frontier's seven facilities. Talk to the attendant to start a streak of single battles; your team is healed before each one, and a loss just ends the streak. Win 5 in a row and Salon Maiden Anabel battles you for the Silver Symbol, then 10 more for her Gold Symbol team. Every facility here plays this same simple way ours (Part 20). See Battle facilities.

The Battle Dome. Win 5 in a row and Dome Ace Tucker battles you for the Silver Symbol; then 10 in a row for the Gold. Wins earn Battle Points. See Battle facilities.

The Battle Palace. Win 5 in a row and Palace Maven Spenser battles you for the Silver Symbol; then 10 in a row for the Gold. Wins earn Battle Points. See Battle facilities.

The Battle Pyramid. Win 5 in a row and Pyramid King Brandon, with his Regirock, Regice and Registeel, battles you for the Silver Symbol; then 10 in a row for the Gold. Wins earn Battle Points. See Battle facilities.

The Battle Arena. Win 5 in a row and Arena Tycoon Greta battles you for the Silver Symbol; then 10 in a row for the Gold. Wins earn Battle Points. See Battle facilities.

The Battle Factory. Win 5 in a row and Factory Head Noland battles you with three random strong Pokémon, for the Silver Symbol; then 10 in a row for the Gold. Wins earn Battle Points. See Battle facilities.

The Battle Pike. Win 5 in a row and Pike Queen Lucy battles you for the Silver Symbol; then 10 in a row for the Gold. Wins earn Battle Points. See Battle facilities.

The Frontier's Pokémon Center. The Judge sits here: he looks at your first Pokémon and tells you how much potential it has (Part 20).

The Frontier's Poké Mart sells strong goods only: Ultra Balls, Hyper Potions, Max Potions, Full Restores, Full Heals, Revives, Max Repels and X Attacks.

The Ranking Hall, where boards show the records of the Frontier's facilities.

The Exchange Service Corner, where you spend Battle Points on Emerald's prizes: vitamins for 1 BP each, and held items like Leftovers, Choice Band and Focus Band for 48 or 64 BP (Part 20). See Battle facilities.

A floor of the Battle Tower that doesn't officially exist, with a Game Corner of its own and a crowd of trainers playing the slots ours. See Game Corners.

The Battle Maison, from Omega Ruby and Alpha Sapphire's Battle Resort, has moved into the Frontier ours. It works like the other facilities: single battles, your team healed in between. Win 5 in a row and the Chatelaine, Nita, battles you in the battle room at the back; then 10 in a row for her stronger team. It gives no Symbol of its own (Part 20). See Battle facilities.

The Battle Maison's battle room, where Chatelaine Nita waits for trainers on a winning streak.
