Introduction

Sinnoh is a cold region in the north, split down the middle by a great mountain, Mt. Coronet. Three lakes sit around it, each said to be home to a mysterious Pokémon. You live in Twinleaf Town, a little snowy town in the southwest, right next door to your rival, a boy who never walks anywhere when he can run. Professor Rowan, the Pokémon Professor, has his Lab in Sandgem Town, just to the east.

There are two ways to get here:

  • A new game. Pick Sinnoh from the region list. Choose your look and type your name. Your rival is the boy next door, and you give him his name.
  • From another region. Once you become the Champion of the region you're in, Mom wants to spend your prize money on a new home, and asks where to move. Pick Sinnoh (she only offers regions you haven't played yet). Mom finds a new home in Twinleaf Town, and you name your rival when you arrive. Sinnoh keeps its own party, PC and Bag, so you arrive with no Pokémon and get your first partner the same way as in a new game, at Lake Verity.

Either way, your Journal (in the MENU) always shows your next main mission, and a gold marker on the map points the way.

How this guide plays

This guide is written for two settings in Options › Gameplay: Battle style: Overworld and Level cap: Story. The cap is on from the start; the battle style you switch on yourself.

Overworld battles

On routes, in caves and in places like the Valley Windworks, you can see the wild Pokémon moving about, mostly in the tall grass. Walk into one and the battle happens right there on the map: it fights from where it stands, yours steps up beside it, and small HP bars float over both. Trainers on these maps spot you and walk over. Their words come up in the map's text box, they send out their Pokémon in front of them, and you fight on the map too. In the trainer tables, "on the map" marks these.

Every battle still works the same way: pick a move and answer its math question to land it. The buttons and questions are the usual ones.

The important story battles always open the battle screen: Gym Leaders, Team Galactic's commanders and its boss, your rival, the Elite Four and the Champion. So do the trainers inside Gyms and towns, story scenes and a bite on your rod. Each battle box says which.

Morning, day and night

Some Pokémon only come out at certain times of day: Kricketot only come out at night, for one. Where a place's Pokémon change with the time, the tables give a rate for the morning, the day and the night.

The Poké Radar and swarms

Much later, once you have the National Pokédex, Roseanne, an aide in Professor Rowan's Lab in Sandgem Town, gives you the Poké Radar. Use it in tall grass (from the Bag, or the RADAR button) and patches of grass nearby start to shake: walk into one and you may meet a Pokémon that doesn't live there otherwise. The tables list these Pokémon under "Poké Radar". From then on, a swarm fills one place's grass each day too, and a child in Sandgem Town hears about it on the TV news.

The story level cap

Your Pokémon can't level past the next Gym Leader's best Pokémon plus two. At the cap they stop gaining Exp., and Rare Candies don't work. Beat the Leader and the cap moves up. Each part starts with a box giving the cap for that stretch.

Levels never rise to meet you: wild Pokémon always come at their place's own levels, and trainers keep their own levels. So the levels in this guide's tables and battle boxes are the ones you'll face, however strong your team is.

Poké Marts and prizes

Every Poké Mart in Sinnoh sells from the same counter, and it grows with the Sinnoh badges you hold. TMs join the shelf as you earn badges too, and the Pokémon League's shop adds Revives, Rare Candy, Full Restores and Max Potions. Not every town has a Mart: Twinleaf Town has none, so your first one is in Sandgem Town.

Route trainers don't pay in Poké Balls: beating one gives you a healing prize that grows with your badges.

Twinleaf Town

Twinleaf Town is a small, quiet town in the southwest of Sinnoh, with snow on the rooftops. Your house and your rival's house stand side by side, and there's no Pokémon Center or Poké Mart. The only road out is Route 201, at the north edge of town. The town sign calls it fresh and free.

{{tables:TWINLEAF_TOWN}}

The water can be fished from the shore once you have a rod, and surfed much later. The Odd Keystone is hidden somewhere in town: step on its spot to pick it up. It belongs to a little tower on Route 209.

A red Gyarados

Your story starts on a snowy morning. Your rival bursts in through your front door with big news: there's a red Gyarados at a lake, and he wants to go and look for one just like it. Then he's off again before you can answer.

He isn't at the edge of town, though. If you try to walk out onto Route 201, a man calls you back: your rival was just here looking for you, then ran home, so go and see him at his house first.

Your rival's house

Your rival's mom says you've just missed him again. He ran off in a hurry, saying something about Lake Verity, and Route 201 is just north of town. She hopes he remembered his shoes.

{{journal:sinnoh_m1}}

Your house

Talk to Mom any time you're home and she heals your team. She has something different to say each time, like reminding you to wrap up warm for the lake, or that Cynthia, the Champion, was on TV this morning. If you moved here from another region, she talks about your new snowy cottage instead.

Around town

A man says one adventure can make a child come home a whole new person, a woman tells you that rough wild Pokémon can still become your friends, and a big man is amazed you can link up with people far away. In the houses, a neighbour once went on an adventure all the way to Sandgem Town, a grandma wonders what the Professor found in the four years he was away, and a little girl wants a cute Pokémon.

Route 201

Route 201 is a short, grassy road north of Twinleaf Town. It runs east to Sandgem Town and west to the Verity Lakefront and Lake Verity. Ledges let you hop down quickly toward Twinleaf, but not climb back up.

You can't catch anything yet, since you have no Pokémon, no Pokédex and no Poké Balls. Once you have a partner, battling the wild Pokémon here is a good way to level it up.

{{tables:SINNOH_ROUTE_201}}

On a swarm day (after the National Pokédex), Doduo fill the grass here.

Free sample

A worker from the Poké Mart stands on the route. Talk to the worker for a free Potion: it goes in your Bag's Medicine pocket. After that the worker just tells you there's a Poké Mart in most towns.

Around the route, a boy points out that the ledge is a quick way home to Twinleaf, a man explains that wild Pokémon hide in tall grass (walk through it for a battle, go around it to avoid one), and a girl reminds you that a Pokémon Center fixes up worn-out Pokémon. The sign shows the way: Lake Verity to the west, Sandgem Town to the east.

Verity Lakefront

The Verity Lakefront is a little stretch of ground at the west end of Route 201, on the way to Lake Verity. Its sign calls Lake Verity the lake of emotion. Head north into the lake.

{{tables:VERITY_LAKEFRONT}}

A Tiny Mushroom is hidden here: step on its spot to find it.

Lake Verity

Lake Verity is a quiet lake ringed by trees, with tall grass along the shore. Out in the middle of the water there's a small island with a cave, Verity Cavern, but you can't reach it for a long time.

{{tables:LAKE_VERITY}}

The water can be surfed and fished much later. Team Galactic only comes here much later in the story, after Lake Valor (see Part 11), and Verity Cavern opens even later than that.

The briefcase

Your rival is already at the lake, still thinking about that red Gyarados: there must be a Pokémon like that in their own lake too. By the water stand an old man and his young assistant, staring out over the lake. It's Professor Rowan. They hurry off, and leave a briefcase behind in the grass.

Just then, wild Starly swoop down out of the trees! Your rival spots the briefcase: there are Poké Balls inside, so pick one, quick!

Look at each Pokémon in the briefcase, then answer the math questions to choose your partner.

Turtwig

Grass. Strong against Roark's Rock types at the first Gym. A good, easy start.

Chimchar

Fire. Weak against Roark's Rock types, so the first Gym is the hardest with it, but strong against Gardenia's Grass types at the second.

Piplup

Water. Strong against Roark, and a steady pick all the way through.

Your new partner goes straight into battle against a wild Starly (Lv 2). You can't catch it. Beat it and it flies off.

Professor Rowan comes back for his briefcase. He sees that you used one of his Pokémon, thinks for a moment, and tells you to come and see him at his Lab in Sandgem Town. Your rival is just relieved, and wants to head home first: he's itching for a battle.

Rival battle 1

As soon as you're back in town, your rival is waiting. He's been training so hard, he says, he's going to be the best Trainer in Sinnoh, but first he has to beat you. He picked the Pokémon that has the type advantage over yours.
