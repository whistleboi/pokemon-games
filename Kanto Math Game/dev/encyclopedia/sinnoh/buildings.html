Your home in Twinleaf Town, right next door to your rival's. Your Sinnoh story starts here, when your rival bursts in with news of a red Gyarados. Talk to Mom whenever you're home and she heals your team; she always has something new to say. If you moved here from another region, this is your new snowy cottage. See Part 1.

Your rival's house, next door to yours. At the very start of the story you have to look in here before the man at the edge of town lets you onto Route 201: your rival's mom says he's just run off toward Lake Verity (Part 1).

Professor Rowan's Lab, in the middle of Sandgem Town. Your first visit is a big story moment: the Professor sees how happy your new partner is and gives you your Pokédex (Part 2). Two aides work here. Much later, once you have the National Pokédex, the aide Roseanne gives you the Poké Radar, which shakes the grass to bring out Pokémon that don't live there otherwise.

The home of Dawn, Professor Rowan's assistant, next to the Lab. Talk to Dawn here and she sends you to Route 202, where she teaches you to catch Pokémon. Her little sibling becomes useful much later: once you have the National Pokédex, they tell you which place is having today's swarm, straight from the TV news. See Part 2.

The first Pokémon Center on your journey. Healing is free; upstairs and downstairs, the link rooms are all closed.

Your first Poké Mart. Every Mart in Sinnoh sells from the same counter, and it stocks more as you win badges.

The company that makes the Pokétch, the Pokémon Watch, in the middle of Jubilife City. Its President is giving watches away: find the three clowns hidden around town, answer each one's question for a Pokétch Coupon, and bring all three here for your own Pokétch. You can't leave Jubilife to the east until you have it. See Part 2.

The Global Terminal, where Trainers trade with the whole world. Its guide won't let you in until you have a Gym Badge, and even then the Global Trade Station inside isn't open: the link isn't finished. The people on its floors chat about trading and ranked battles. See Part 2 and Part 4.

A house four floors high. On the ground floor a lady gives you a Quick Claw; climb all the way to the top and another lady, impressed by your focus, gives you a Focus Band. On the floors in between, people talk about Heal Balls, new moves and trading. See Part 2.

A block of flats where the neighbours talk about Pokémon, trading and nicknames. A helpful girl says that if you're ever stuck, talk to everyone and go everywhere.

The Trainers' School. The teacher and students share easy lessons: what a Potion does, how Pokémon use held Berries by themselves, and the Grass, Fire and Water triangle. See Part 2.

In this house a boy traded for a Pikachu that grows fast but doesn't always listen.

The top floor of Jubilife TV, where the station's president thinks a fine Trainer like you belongs on their shows.

Jubilife TV, the city's TV station. Its Lottery Corner on the ground floor isn't open yet, the second floor has a fitting room and a studio where you can dress up a Pokémon, and the third has records rooms whose machines are still being tuned. The station's president is on 4F. See Part 2.

Gardenia's Grass-type Gym is a garden built like a clock. The Gym Guide's tip: Grass types hate fire. Win the Forest Badge and TM081 Grass Knot; then Cynthia, by the statue in town, gives you HM Cut. See Part 5 and Gyms.

Eterna's bike shop. When you first visit it's short-handed: the manager went to the Team Galactic building and hasn't come back. Clear the Galactic Eterna Building and Rad Rickshaw thanks you with a Bicycle, which you need for Cycling Road on Route 206. See Part 5.

A block of flats with some useful neighbours. The Name Rater lives on the ground floor and rates or changes your Pokémon's nicknames. A girl there will trade her Chatot, holding a Leppa Berry, for a Buizel. Upstairs, an old lady gives you TM301 Recycle. See Part 5.

Eterna's herb shop. It sells Heal Powder, Energy Powder, Energy Root and Revival Herb: strong medicine, but bitter, so your Pokémon won't love it. See Part 5.

Hearthome is famous for Pokémon Contests, and this is where they're held. Your Pokémon is judged on its condition first, then makes five appeals with its moves, each landed by answering a maths problem ours. Win a rank and the next one opens. Upstairs, three coaches give tips: treats that suit the contest, no move twice in a row, and quick answers to the Judges' sums. Fantina, the Gym Leader, is here watching the shows on your first visit. See Part 6.

Fantina's Ghost-type Gym is dark, and its doors are a maths test ours. In each of the two trainer rooms a voice asks you a sum, and each door holds one answer: pick the right one and it leads on; pick a wrong one and you're back at the entrance. The first room has three doors, the second five, with a harder question. Fantina plays a game with you before she battles ours. She only opens the Gym once you hold four badges. Win the Relic Badge and TM061 Shadow Claw. See Part 9 and Gyms.

Part of Fantina's Hearthome Gym. A voice asks you a sum, and the right one of the three doors (circle, square and triangle) leads on ours; a wrong door sends you back to the entrance.

Part of Fantina's Hearthome Gym. A harder sum, and five doors this time: sun, donut, moon, star and heart ours.

Part of Fantina's Hearthome Gym. Fantina waits here and plays a game with you before she battles ours. Once she's beaten, the way to the warp panel back to the entrance opens.
